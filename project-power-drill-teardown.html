Engineering Design (Tufts) — Spring 2026 — Team Project

Cordless Hammer Drill Teardown & Reverse-Engineered Assembly

A full disassembly, CAD reconstruction, and documentation of a 20V cordless hammer drill, covering the shell, battery housing, controls, geartrain, motor, chuck, and impact mechanism.

Tools SolidWorks
Deliverables Full Assembly Model Bill of Materials Exploded View Function Structure Diagram Video Walkthrough

Project Overview

For this team project, we tore down a DCA 20V cordless hammer drill and reconstructed the full assembly in CAD, including a bill of materials and exploded view. As a team, we disassembled the drill together and did background research on how it worked, then split up the remaining components to model individually.

Engineering Approach

As a team, we disassembled the drill piece by piece, reasoning through each part's function as we removed it — for example, thinking through what a part must be doing based on what stopped working once it came out. This got harder once we removed the case and battery, since at that point the drill could no longer run, so understanding how the remaining parts interacted became more of a thought experiment than something we could test directly. We also mapped out a function structure diagram, tracing how energy and information flowed through the drill from trigger input to rotational output. Once we had a working understanding of the system, we split up the remaining CAD modeling: I modeled the outer shell, the battery housing, the electronics and controls (gear selector, direction switch, trigger), and the planetary gear train.

Design Iteration

Rather than each person building an isolated subassembly, I took on assembling the entire drill myself, importing CAD models that teammates had built for the motor, chuck, and impact mechanism into my own shell, controls, and gear train models. This meant working with parts I hadn't designed myself — in a few cases, proportions on imported components (like the motor rotor and housing) didn't quite line up with how they needed to fit into the shell, so I had to edit and adjust models I hadn't originally built. That process taught me how to read and interpret someone else's modeling choices and feature structure.

Since the drill used relatively few screws, figuring out which parts were meant to press directly against each other, rather than being fastened, also required some guesswork when setting up mates, based on the geometry and how the parts had fit together during teardown.

Final Outcome

The completed deliverables included a full assembly model, bill of materials, exploded view, function structure diagram, and a final video walkthrough of the disassembled and reconstructed drill.
