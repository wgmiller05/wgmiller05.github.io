CAD Design Course (Tufts) — Spring 2026 — Individual Project

Manual Coffee Grinder — Reverse-Engineered Assembly

A full reverse-engineered CAD model and assembly of a manual coffee grinder, built entirely from physical measurements rather than existing drawings.

Tools SolidWorks
Deliverables Full CAD Assembly Assembly Drawing Part Drawings Animation

Project Overview

For this individual final project, I had to reverse-engineer a real object into a complete CAD assembly of at least 6 unique components, including a dynamic (moving) element, and produce full engineering drawings and an animation. I chose a manual coffee grinder — I'd originally considered a brake caliper, but wanted a subject I could actually purchase and physically measure myself, rather than relying on estimated proportions or hard-to-find reference drawings. No published drawings for this specific grinder exist, so every dimension had to come from direct measurement.

Engineering Approach

Since there were no existing engineering drawings to reference, I used calipers to measure every component directly from the physical grinder. Where two parts connected or shared a dimension, I reused the already-measured value rather than re-measuring, to keep the parts consistent with each other. I started by modeling the outer shell first, then built the remaining components around it so everything would mate correctly in the final assembly.

Design Iteration

Some internal features couldn't be measured directly with calipers, since I couldn't always reach inside the assembled grinder. In those cases, I used engineering judgment to infer reasonable dimensions based on how the parts needed to fit together.

The most challenging part to figure out was the fineness adjustment mechanism: understanding how it actually worked mechanically, then translating that into SolidWorks mates that reproduced the same motion, took the most iteration of any part of the model. Turning the adjustment ring doesn't move the ring itself linearly — the ring's internal threads instead drive the inner shaft up and down as it rotates. I modeled that with a concentric mate for the ring's rotation and a screw mate to couple that rotation to the inner shaft's linear travel, matching the thread pitch I'd modeled. To keep the adjustment from over-rotating past its physical limits, I added both a limit angle mate and a limit distance mate, with the limits calculated directly from the thread pitch rather than set arbitrarily.

Key Challenges

Measuring a fully assembled physical object without disassembling it meant some dimensions had to be inferred rather than measured directly — a few early guesses didn't make sense once I tried to mate the parts together, so I had to revisit and correct them. The fineness adjustment mechanism was the hardest part to reverse-engineer: I was able to model its overall motion successfully using mates, but I wasn't able to fully capture the ball detent mechanism that gives the adjustment its "click" positions.

Final Outcome

The final deliverable included a complete multi-part assembly, a published assembly drawing with exploded view and BOM, part drawings for select components, and an animation showing the assembly sequence followed by the grinder's adjustment mechanism in motion.
